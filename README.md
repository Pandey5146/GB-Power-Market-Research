# GB Power Market Extreme Price Regime Analysis, 2023–2025

This project analyses extreme price formation in the Great Britain power market using half-hourly system price, fuel mix, imbalance, interconnector and REMIT context data.

The research asks whether high-price spikes and negative-price events are caused by single drivers, or whether they emerge from wider market regimes involving wind output, gas generation, imbalance direction, interconnector flows and asset-availability context.

## Key findings

- Extreme prices were regime-dependent rather than random.
- 2023 upper-tail stress was concentrated in a Q1 compound scarcity regime.
- 2024 stress was more selective and late-year focused.
- 2025 showed mixed-tail behaviour, combining a £2,900/MWh positive price event with frequent negative-price periods.
- Single drivers such as low wind or high gas were not enough on their own.
- Spike probability increased sharply when multiple conditions aligned, especially low wind, positive imbalance and evening timing.
- REMIT notifications were useful as external context, but should not be treated as simple one-outage explanations.
